# Imaginary-frequency mappings for the RPA correlation energy

Reference document for the grids registered in [`src/xc/rpa_omega_grids.py`](../../src/xc/rpa_omega_grids.py)
and swept by this directory. One section per mapping: the transformation, its Jacobian, the
constants and where they come from, the published reference, the **closed-form location of the
χ₀ poles after the mapping**, and that mapping's measured cost.

---

## Notation

| symbol | meaning |
|---|---|
| ω ∈ [0, ∞) | imaginary frequency, the physical integration variable |
| ω̃ ∈ [−1, 1] | reference variable in which the base rule is defined |
| w | quadrature weights in ω |
| w̃ | weights of the base rule on [−1, 1] |
| c | scale constant of a mapping |
| p | degree (order) of the algebraic mapping |
| Δ = ε_a − ε_i | transition energy; χ₀ has poles at ω = ±iΔ |
| Δ_min, Δ_max | smallest and largest Δ in the spectrum |
| n | number of frequency points |

The quantity being computed is

$$E_c^{\rm RPA}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{\infty}\!d\omega\;
\mathrm{Tr}\!\left[\ln\left(1-\nu\chi_0(i\omega)\right)+\nu\chi_0(i\omega)\right],
\qquad
\chi_0(i\omega)=\sum_q A_q\,\frac{2\Delta_q}{\Delta_q^{2}+\omega^{2}}$$

so the integrand is analytic on the real ω axis with **poles at ω = ±iΔ**, spread over
Δ ∈ [Δ_min, Δ_max]. For the five test elements Δ_min is the HOMO–LUMO gap (0.10–0.60 Ha) and
Δ_max is the top of the finite-element basis (3.7 × 10⁸ Ha), so the band ratio
R = Δ_max/Δ_min reaches 3 × 10⁹.

## Common structure

Every grid is a **base rule** on [−1, 1] composed with a **mapping** ω(ω̃):

$$\int_0^\infty f(\omega)\,d\omega=\int_{-1}^{1} f\big(\omega(\tilde\omega)\big)\,
\frac{d\omega}{d\tilde\omega}\,d\tilde\omega
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\omega_j=\omega(\tilde\omega_j),\quad
w_j=\tilde w_j\left.\frac{d\omega}{d\tilde\omega}\right|_{\tilde\omega_j}$$

Only the mapping and its Jacobian are listed below; the weights always follow from this rule.
Keeping the two axes independent is deliberate — several grids described in the literature as
distinct methods are the *same* mapping at a different c, or the same mapping under a different
base rule.

**Why the pole locus is the whole story.** The base rules here are exact for a class of
functions analytic in some region of the ω̃ plane, and the convergence rate is governed by how
far the nearest singularity of the transplanted integrand sits from [−1, 1] — a Bernstein
ellipse for Gauss/Clenshaw–Curtis, a strip for the trapezoidal rule. The mapping's only job is
to push the images of ω = ±iΔ as far from [−1, 1] as possible, **uniformly in Δ**. A map that
does this for one Δ but not another needs a node count that grows with the width of the band,
which is exactly the observed growth with Z.

# 1. Rational / algebraic mapping of degree p

$$\omega=c\left(\frac{1+\tilde\omega}{1-\tilde\omega}\right)^{\!p},
\qquad
\frac{d\omega}{d\tilde\omega}=2cp\,\frac{(1+\tilde\omega)^{p-1}}{(1-\tilde\omega)^{p+1}}$$

**Constants.** c sets the frequency scale; p concentrates nodes toward large ω.

**Poles.** Setting ω = iΔ gives u = ((1+ω̃)/(1−ω̃)) = (iΔ/c)^{1/p}, with p branches, so

$$\boxed{\;\tilde\omega_{\rm pole}
=\frac{(\Delta/c)^{1/p}\,e^{\,i(\pi/2+2\pi k)/p}-1}
{(\Delta/c)^{1/p}\,e^{\,i(\pi/2+2\pi k)/p}+1}\;},
\qquad k=0,\dots,p-1$$

For **p = 1** this has a closed form on the unit circle:

$$\tilde\omega_{\rm pole}=\frac{i\Delta-c}{i\Delta+c}
=\exp\!\Big\{i\big[\pi-2\arctan(\Delta/c)\big]\Big\},
\qquad |\tilde\omega_{\rm pole}|=1\ \text{exactly}$$

The pole angle sweeps from ω̃ = −1 for Δ ≪ c, through **ω̃ = i exactly at Δ = c** (the farthest
possible point, Bernstein radius ρ = 1 + √2), to ω̃ = +1 for Δ ≫ c. For p ≥ 2 the locus is a
circular arc through ±1 meeting the real axis at angle π/2p, so its maximum height falls as

| p | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| max Im ω̃ | 1.00 | 0.41 | 0.27 | 0.20 |

which is why high p fails outright.

**Inverse-linear is this mapping, not a separate family.** The form used in production,
ω = (1 − ω̃)/(1 + ω̃), is p = 1 with c = 1 under ω̃ → −ω̃, giving the same unit-circle locus
mirrored. Verified: `gl_invlin` and `gl_rational_p1_a1` agree to 8.9 × 10⁻¹⁶ in the nodes and
3.4 × 10⁻¹² in the weights. The same map at three scales appears in three places:

| c | source | preset |
|---|---|---|
| 2.5 | PRL **134**, 016402 supplemental | `gl_rational_p1_a2.5` |
| 1 | Boqin MATLAB — the active path in `OneAtomFEM/basic_ingredients.py`, and the SPARC-atomSFE default | `gl_invlin` |
| 0.5 | Ren *et al.*, New J. Phys. **14**, 053020 (2012) — FHI-aims "modified GL" | `gl_rational_p1_a0.5` |

**Measured** — nodes for 1e-5 (1e-4):

| preset | He | Ar | Zn | Mo | Au |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `cc_rational_p1_a2.5` | 15 (10) | 80 (50) | 150 (80) | 150 (80) | 200 (150) |
| `gl_rational_p1_a2.5` | 30 (15) | 150 (80) | 200 (80) | 200 (150) | 300 (200) |
| `cc_rational_p1_a1` | 30 (15) | 150 (80) | 200 (100) | 200 (150) | 300 (200) |
| `gl_invlin` = `gl_rational_p1_a1` | 50 (20) | 200 (100) | 300 (150) | 300 (200) | 400 (300) |
| `gl_rational_p1_a0.5` | 50 (30) | 300 (150) | 400 (200) | 400 (300) | 400 (300) |
| p = 2, 3, 4 (both rules) | — | — | — | — | wrong limit |

Cost grows roughly linearly with Z, and increasing c monotonically helps (0.5 → 1 → 2.5 improves
at every element), consistent with the optimum sitting at c ≈ the geometric centre of the band.
The p ≥ 2 grids converge smoothly to a value 5 × 10⁻⁴ to 3 × 10⁻³ away from consensus and are
excluded from all rankings.

# 2. Tangent mapping

$$\omega=c\tan\frac{\pi(1+\tilde\omega)}{4},
\qquad
\frac{d\omega}{d\tilde\omega}=\frac{c\pi}{4}\sec^{2}\frac{\pi(1+\tilde\omega)}{4}$$

**Poles.** With θ = π(1+ω̃)/4, ω = iΔ requires tan θ = iΔ/c, i.e. θ = i·artanh(Δ/c):

$$\boxed{\;\tilde\omega_{\rm pole}=
\begin{cases}
-1+\dfrac{4i}{\pi}\,\mathrm{artanh}\!\left(\Delta/c\right), & \Delta<c\\[3mm]
+1+\dfrac{4i}{\pi}\,\mathrm{artanh}\!\left(c/\Delta\right), & \Delta>c
\end{cases}\;}$$

The poles sit on the **vertical lines Re ω̃ = ∓1**, at a height that vanishes as Δ/c → 0 or ∞.
Structurally the same defect as the algebraic map: the locus is anchored to the interval
endpoints, so extreme Δ always crowds one of them.

**Measured:** `cc_tan_a2.5` 20 (15) → 200 (150) He→Au; `gl_tan_a1` 50 (30) → 400 (300).
Comparable to the algebraic map at the same c, with no advantage.

---

# 3. Logarithmic mapping — *withdrawn*

$$\omega=-c\ln\frac{1-\tilde\omega}{2},
\qquad
\frac{d\omega}{d\tilde\omega}=\frac{c}{1-\tilde\omega}$$

**Poles.**

$$\boxed{\;\tilde\omega_{\rm pole}=1-2e^{-i\left(\Delta/c+2\pi k\right)},\qquad k\in\mathbb{Z}\;}$$

The **circle |ω̃ − 1| = 2** — and because of the 2πk the image *wraps around it repeatedly* as Δ
grows, so pole images accumulate densely on a curve that passes through ω̃ = −1.

**Why withdrawn.** The reach is only ω ~ c·ln(2/(1−ω̃)), so at n = 40 the largest node is ω ≈ 7
(c = 1) or 17 (c = 2.5) against a band ending at 3.7 × 10⁸. All four presets converged to
visibly wrong limits for He: −0.080177 and −0.083073 (CC), −0.080572 and −0.083203 (GL), against
the consensus −0.084310, i.e. 1.3–4.9 % off, and were still drifting at n = 400. The mapping
remains available in `build_omega_grid` for exponentially decaying integrands.

---

# 4. Log-uniform mapping

$$\omega=e^{t},\quad
t=\ln\omega_{\min}+\tfrac12(1+\tilde\omega)L,\quad
L=\ln\frac{\omega_{\max}}{\omega_{\min}},
\qquad
\frac{d\omega}{d\tilde\omega}=\frac{\omega L}{2}$$

Nodes equispaced in ln ω, so every decade of the band is resolved equally.

**Poles.** ω = iΔ requires t = ln Δ + i(π/2 + 2πk):

$$\boxed{\;\tilde\omega_{\rm pole}=-1+\frac{2}{L}
\left[\ln\frac{\Delta}{\omega_{\min}}+i\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+2\pi k\right)\right]\;}$$

This is the **first mapping with a Δ-independent locus**: a horizontal line at

$$\operatorname{Im}\tilde\omega=\frac{\pi}{L}$$

the same height for every pole. That is why its cost stops growing with Z. But with
ω_min = 10⁻⁹ and ω_max = 10⁷, L = 36.8 and the strip is only 0.085 wide — flat, but thin.

**Measured:** 80 (50) at He, Ar, Zn, Mo and 50 (30) at Au — the only grid that gets *cheaper*
with Z, because a fixed decade span suits a wide band better than a narrow one.

---

# 5. Double-exponential mapping (`de_sinh`)

$$\omega=\omega_{\rm geo}\,e^{\,c\sinh(U\tilde\omega)},
\qquad \omega_{\rm geo}=\sqrt{\omega_{\min}\omega_{\max}},
\qquad
\frac{d\omega}{d\tilde\omega}=\omega\,cU\cosh(U\tilde\omega)$$

Two constants: c sets how many decades are spanned, U where the sinh saturates; both are fixed
from the requested band. exp() covers the decades while sinh() makes **both** tails decay
doubly exponentially, so ω → 0 and ω → ∞ each cost O(1) nodes.

**Poles.**

$$\boxed{\;\tilde\omega_{\rm pole}=\frac{1}{U}\,
\mathrm{arcsinh}\!\left[\frac{\ln(\Delta/\omega_{\rm geo})+i(\pi/2+2\pi k)}{c}\right]\;}$$

For large |ln Δ| the arcsinh flattens and Im ω̃ ≈ π/(2cU|ln Δ|), so the locus **approaches the
real axis logarithmically** — measured 0.141 → 0.058 across seven decades of Δ. Better than
log-uniform near the band centre, worse at its edges.

**Reference.** Takahasi & Mori, Publ. RIMS Kyoto Univ. **9** (1974) 721–741; see also
Trefethen & Weideman, SIAM Review **56** (2014) 385–458, Table 14.1, where the canonical form is
ξ = tanh(½π sinh x) with rate exp(−π²n/log(4πn)).

**Measured:** `gl_desinh` / `cc_desinh` 50 nodes at *every* element; `mp_desinh` 30. The base
rule matters here — the DE family is defined with the trapezoidal rule, and pairing it with
Gauss–Legendre costs a factor 1.7.

# 6. Sinh mapping (Johnston–Elliott), c = Δ_min

Natural variable y ∈ [0, y_max] with y = ½(1+ω̃)y_max and y_max = arcsinh(ω_max/c):

$$\omega=c\sinh y,
\qquad
\frac{d\omega}{dy}=c\cosh y,
\qquad
\frac{d\omega}{d\tilde\omega}=\frac{y_{\max}}{2}\,c\cosh y$$

**Reference.** Johnston & Elliott, *A sinh transformation for evaluating nearly singular
boundary element integrals*, Int. J. Numer. Meth. Engng **62** (2005) 564–578. Their
transformation for a singularity at A + Bi on an interval [a, b] is

$$x(t)=A+B\sinh\!\left[\frac{1-t}{2}\,\mathrm{arcsinh}\frac{a-A}{B}
+\frac{1+t}{2}\,\mathrm{arcsinh}\frac{b-A}{B}\right]$$

derived from the boundary-value problem x′(t) = k√((x−A)² + B²), i.e. *keep the derivative small
where x is near the singularity*. Our case is A = 0, B = Δ_min, [a, b] = [0, ω_max], which
reduces exactly to the above **including the arcsinh range**: c = Δ_min and y_max are the
recipe's prescription, not free parameters.

**Poles.** Using sinh(u ± iπ/2) = ±i cosh u:

$$\boxed{\;y_{\rm pole}=\pm\,\mathrm{arccosh}\frac{\Delta}{c}+\frac{i\pi}{2}
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
\tilde\omega_{\rm pole}=-1+\frac{2}{y_{\max}}
\left[\mathrm{arccosh}\frac{\Delta}{c}+\frac{i\pi}{2}\right]\;}$$

**Im y = π/2 exactly, for every Δ** — the strip is both Δ-independent *and* the widest of any
mapping here, and it is c = Δ_min that puts the nearest pole precisely on its edge. In
normalised units Im ω̃ = π/y_max = 0.189, versus 0.085 for log-uniform.

Because the transplanted integrand is then analytic in a strip of half-width π/2, the
trapezoidal rule converges as exp(−2πa/h) = **exp(−π²n/y_max)** with y_max only logarithmic in
the band — hence a node count essentially independent of Z. This is the mechanism, and it is a
strip result, not a Bernstein-ellipse result: Gauss–Legendre cannot attain it.

**Measured** — nodes for 1e-5 (1e-4):

| preset | He | Ar | Zn | Mo | Au |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `mp_hht_elliptic` (midpoint) | **15 (15)** | **20 (15)** | **20 (15)** | **20 (15)** | **20 (15)** |
| `cc_hht_elliptic` | 15 (15) | 30 (15) | 30 (30) | 30 (20) | 30 (30) |
| `gl_hht_elliptic` | 15 (10) | 30 (20) | 30 (30) | 30 (20) | 30 (30) |

Flat in Z, and 20× cheaper than the production default at Au.

> **Naming.** The preset is still registered as `hht_elliptic`, which credits Hale, Higham &
> Trefethen. That is wrong: their map is a Möbius transform of sn on a closed contour with
> k = (√(M/m)−1)/(√(M/m)+1). What is taken from them is the *principles* — an elliptic-type map
> for singularities confined to a band, the ln(4R) scaling, and the requirement of an equispaced
> companion rule. The map itself is Johnston & Elliott's. Rename pending.

**The reach must be limited.** ω_max is not Δ_max. Taking ω_max = Δ_max = 3.7 × 10⁸ makes the
rule **stall at ~10⁻³**, because the solver's χ₀ ~ 1/ω² has decayed into numerical noise there
while the Jacobian weight is enormous. Measured against consensus:

| reach | He | Au |
|---|---|---|
| 6.5 × 10⁴ | — | 3.0 × 10⁻⁴ |
| 8.6 × 10⁵ | **1.9 × 10⁻⁷** | 6.0 × 10⁻⁶ |
| 2.5 × 10⁶ | 1.6 × 10⁻⁶ | **4.3 × 10⁻⁶** |
| 6.0 × 10⁸ | 1.0 × 10⁻³ | 7.9 × 10⁻⁶ |

Hence `omega_ceiling = 1e6`, an **absolute** frequency. Tying the reach to Δ_max instead makes
it Z-dependent and gives the widest band the narrowest grid. Trefethen & Weideman §14 document
this floating-point limit as the standing drawback of exponential and double-exponential rules.
This ceiling is the one parameter here not fixed by a published prescription.

# 7. Elliptic sc mapping — *present but never exercised*

$$\omega=c\,\frac{\mathrm{sn}(y\,|\,m)}{\mathrm{cn}(y\,|\,m)},
\qquad k=\frac{\Delta_{\min}}{\Delta_{\max}},\quad m=1-k^{2},\quad c=\Delta_{\min}$$

**Poles.** The locus is the horizontal line

$$\boxed{\;\operatorname{Im}y_{\rm pole}=K'(m)=K(k^{2}),\qquad
\Delta\in[\Delta_{\min},\Delta_{\max}]\;}$$

verified to ≤ 2.5 × 10⁻¹⁴ at k = 10⁻³, 0.3 and 0.5 — and it correctly **fails outside the design
band**: at k = 0.3 with Δ = 20 > Δ_max = c/k the root jumps to a periodic image.

Since K′(m) → π/2 as k → 0, this reduces to §6. With k ≈ 10⁻⁹–10⁻¹⁰ for all five elements,
1 − k² is not representable in double precision, and the implementation always takes the sinh
branch — the elliptic form is never evaluated. Its only advantage would appear for a band narrow
enough that m is representable, which does not occur here.

*Historical note:* an earlier version parameterised the modulus as k → 0 with K from `ellipk(1−k²)`,
which returns ∞ once k < 10⁻⁸ and produced NaN for every element on the true band. The fix is
`ellipkm1(k²)` plus the exact k → 0 closed form.

---

# 8. Gauss–Laguerre — *withdrawn*

No mapping: the rule is native to [0, ∞) with weight e^{−x}, which it divides back out, so the
weights carry e^{+x}. The RPA integrand decays only algebraically (~ω⁻⁴ once the +νχ₀
counterterm cancels the leading order), so convergence was far too slow (−0.084296 at n = 150
against −0.0843102) and for n ≥ 200 the weights overflow to non-finite E_c.

---

# 9. AAA — no mapping at all

Method of Horning & Trefethen, *Quadrature formulas from rational approximations*, IMA J. Numer.
Anal. (2026), doi:10.1093/imanum/draf138. For I = ∫_γ f(z)w(z)dz they form the Cauchy transform
of the **weight**,

$$\mathcal{C}(s)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_\gamma\frac{w(z)\,dz}{s-z},
\qquad I=\int_\Gamma f(s)\,\mathcal{C}(s)\,ds$$

AAA-approximate r_n(s) ≈ 2πi·𝒞(s) on an enclosing contour Γ, and read off

$$r_n(s)=c_\infty+\sum_k\frac{c_k}{s-z_k}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
I_n=\sum_k c_k\,f(z_k)$$

so the **poles are the nodes and the residues are the weights**. AAA never sees f; it enters only
by being evaluated at the nodes and by constraining where Γ may lie.

Three requirements, each established by measurement:

1. **Not in ω.** There f is analytic only for |Im ω| < Δ_min while the arc runs to ω_max, so Γ
   needs half-width Δ_min/ω_max ≈ 6 × 10⁻⁸ against 0.22 in the paper's Fig. 3. The two sides of
   Γ then differ by the branch jump 2πi — the two-branch problem their §8 says plain AAA handles
   unreliably. Confirmed: the fit residual sticks at O(1) for every degree from 20 to 150.
2. **Transform first.** Under §6's map the poles move to Im y = ±π/2 against y_max ≈ 16.6, i.e.
   ε ≈ 0.085 — the same difficulty as their benchmark.
3. **Keep w = 1, put the Jacobian in f.** Setting w = c·cosh y fails badly (100 % error at
   Δ = 0.5): cosh grows like e^y, so AAA spends every node at large ω. **AAA optimises for the
   weight, not for where f is large.** With w = 1 the target is their §3 logarithm,
   2πi𝒞(s) = log(s/(s − y_max)).

Nodes come out slightly complex; taking real parts costs nothing measurable (1.98 × 10⁻⁶ →
1.62 × 10⁻⁶ at degree 30) and is required because the solver evaluates χ₀ at real frequencies.

**Measured against the midpoint rule on the same map** (synthetic ω⁻² kernel, Au band, worst case
over Δ = 0.5 … 10⁵, error against the truncated-arc exact value):

| nodes | AAA | midpoint |
|---|---|---|
| ~20 | 1.4 × 10⁻⁴ | 1.9 × 10⁻³ |
| ~25 | 2.4 × 10⁻⁵ | 1.2 × 10⁻³ |
| ~30 | **2.0 × 10⁻⁶** | 8.4 × 10⁻⁴ |
| ~40–50 | **1.0 × 10⁻⁸** | 3.0 × 10⁻⁴ |

The midpoint rule stagnates because it over-resolves small Δ while never fixing large Δ; AAA
converges uniformly. Its weights come out ≈ y_max/n in the interior with tapered ends — it
rediscovers the trapezoidal rule *plus* the end corrections.

Real runs (preset `aaa_sinh`): He 1.07 × 10⁻⁵ at n = 20 and 3.6 × 10⁻⁸ at n = 30; Au
2.2 × 10⁻⁵ at n = 20 and 3.1 × 10⁻⁸ at n = 30. Full ladder pending.

The AAA implementation is Nakatsukasa, Sète & Trefethen, SIAM J. Sci. Comput. **40** (2018)
A1494, validated against the §3 benchmark of the quadrature paper (1.7 × 10⁻⁵ at degree 20,
against the 1.6 × 10⁻⁴ they report).

# Base rules

| rule | notes |
|---|---|
| `gauss_legendre` | interior nodes; optimal for functions analytic in a Bernstein ellipse |
| `clenshaw_curtis` | keeps one endpoint, giving an explicit ω = 0 node; endpoint weights 1/(m²−1) for even m, 1/m² for odd. One endpoint must be dropped since every mapping sends it to ω = ∞; `drop='low'` for inverse-linear, `'high'` otherwise |
| `midpoint` | equispaced, w̃ = 2/n. Required by §6/§7: the transplanted integrand is periodic-in-a-strip, where the trapezoidal rule converges as exp(−2πa/h). Midpoint rather than closed trapezoid because y = y_max maps to ω = ∞ |
| `gauss_laguerre` | native to [0, ∞), ignores the mapping — withdrawn |
| `aaa` | generates its own nodes and weights, §9 |

Measured effect of the base rule at fixed mapping: 20 vs 30 nodes (§6), 30 vs 50 (§5) — a
consistent 1.5–1.7× for the equispaced rule.

# Summary

Distance of the pole locus from [−1, 1], which predicts the measured ordering exactly:

| mapping | Im ω̃ at the poles | Δ-independent? | Au nodes @1e-5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| sinh (§6) | **π/y_max = 0.189** | **yes** | **20** |
| AAA (§9) | — (no map) | — | ~30 |
| de_sinh (§5) | 0.141 → 0.058 | no, shrinks | 30–50 |
| log-uniform (§4) | π/L = 0.085 | yes | 50–80 |
| rational p=1 (§1) | 1 at Δ = c → 0 at both extremes | no, collapses | 200–400 |
| tan (§2) | → 0 at both extremes | no | 200–400 |
| rational p≥2 (§1) | ≤ 0.41, → 0 | no | wrong limit |
| log (§3) | wraps, accumulates | no | wrong limit |
